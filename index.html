Part 1A • How Listeners Think

Why the "Crocodile Brain" Decides First

When you share an idea at work, you think you are talking to the listener's Logical Brain. Actually, your message goes through their "Crocodile Brain" first—the fast, emotional part of the brain that asks: "Is this safe? Is this boring? Why should I care?"

Key Rule: People hate losing 2× more than they love winning

1. Ignores Boring Starts

A real crocodile ignores things that do not move. If you start a meeting with long company history, people stop listening right away.

2. Hates Complicated Details

Too many numbers or technical words make the brain tired. When a busy boss feels tired or confused, they say "No" just to stop thinking.

3. Gets Angry If Blamed

Never say "You are doing this wrong." That makes people defensive. Instead, blame old systems or old habits (called legacy approaches).

4. Wakes Up to Change + Help

The brain pays attention when it sees: 1) a change in the world, 2) a clear cost, and 3) an easy, safe solution.

Weak Opening (What Most People Say)

"Hi everyone, today I want to show you 30 slides about our company history and software details so you can approve our budget."

Why it fails: It sounds boring, takes too much mental energy, and asks for money too early = The listener stops paying attention.

Step 1 • The Macro Shift
🦎 Gets Attention

"Over the past [6–18 months], the market and operational landscape in [industry] has undergone a major macro shift..."

Simple Explanation (Why it works):

Start by talking about a big change in the outside world. This wakes up the listener's attention immediately without making them feel criticized.

Step 2 • The Cost of Old Habits
⚡ Shows the Problem

"Yet, most organizations are still relying on legacy approaches, leading to severe bottlenecks and [cost / lost time]..."

Simple Explanation (Why it works):

Say "Yet, most organizations still use legacy approaches (old ways)..." This politely shows that doing nothing is losing money, without blaming the listener personally.

Step 3 • The Value Anchor
🧠 1 Clear Number

"Our objective today is to explore how [our solution] eliminates this friction and delivers [1 clear result with a number]."

Simple Explanation (Why it works):

The word "explore" feels friendly and low-pressure. Then, give one clear number (a Value Anchor) so their logical brain wants to hear your plan!

Part 2 • Word Spotlight: "Leverage"

What Does "Leverage" Actually Mean?

The word leverage comes from a lever (a long bar balanced on a point to lift something heavy). In both physics and money, leverage means using a small effort to control something much bigger.

Everyday Business Verb ("to leverage" = to use smartly):

"We can leverage (= use) our current customer list to launch the new product quickly."

1. Leverage Multiplier: 5x (5:1)
1x (No Leverage) 5x 10x 20x (High Risk)
2. Your Own Money (Margin): $1,000
$500 $2,500 $5,000 $10,000
3. Market Price Change: +10%
-20% (Price Drops) 0% +10% +20% (Price Rises)
Picture: How a Lever Multiplies Your Power Balanced Multiplier (5x)
1. In Physics (Lifting a Rock)
Your Push (Effort): 100 kg
Pivot Point (Fulcrum): The Triangle
Bar Multiplier: 5x longer bar
Heavy Rock Lifted: 500 kg
2. In Trading (Multiplying Money)
Your Cash (Margin): $1,000
Borrowed Money: $4,000
Total Trade Size: $5,000
Your Profit or Loss: +$500 (+50%)

How Leverage Multiplies Both Profits AND Losses

With leverage, your profits grow faster, but your losses also grow faster: At 5x leverage, a -20.0% price drop wipes out 100% of your cash!

What is "Liquidation" (or a Margin Call)? At 5x leverage, if the market price drops by -20.0%, you lose 100% of your own money. The broker automatically closes your trade so you don't lose their borrowed money.
